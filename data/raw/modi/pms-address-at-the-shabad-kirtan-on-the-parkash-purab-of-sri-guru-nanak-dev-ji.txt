Guru Pyari Saadh Sangat Ji, Waheguru Ji Ka Khalsa, Waheguru Ji Ki Fateh!

Revered saints of the Sikh community, Lieutenant Governor of Delhi Taranjit Singh Sandhu ji, Chief Minister of Delhi Sister Rekha Gupta ji, my colleagues in Parliament, other ministers of the Delhi Government, all distinguished guests, ladies and gentlemen,

In this sacred atmosphere, I respectfully bow to Guru Nanak Dev Ji. I extend lakh-lakh greetings to all the people of the country on the occasion of Prakash Parv. On this holy occasion of Guru Purab, I sincerely thank Taranjit Singh Sandhu ji for giving me the opportunity to participate in this Shabad Kirtan.

Friends,

Every word of Gurbani and every note of Kirtan is not merely music. It is a form of spiritual practice in itself. It is like diving into an ocean of knowledge. Therefore, whenever I receive an invitation to such an event, whenever I get an opportunity, I make it a point to be present in the Sangat and Kirtan.

Friends,

Guru Nanak Dev Ji used to say: Kirat Karo, Vand Chhako, Naam Japo. These teachings have been a natural part of my life. Bowing my head at Gurdwaras, immersing myself in Gurbani and experiencing spiritual bliss, finding joy in service, and then partaking in Langar, this has been a continuous part of my journey from childhood to my days as an organisational worker. Many times, I have had the good fortune of bowing my head at Sri Harmandir Sahib. Old colleagues know that I spent a considerable amount of time in Punjab as well, and that period remains one of the most special memories of my life.

Brothers and sisters,

By Guru’s grace, I have continued to receive the company of Sikh saints. At the Prime Minister’s residence too, I have had the privilege of hosting many Sikh saints and Gyanis. Even today, the presence of our saints at this gathering is creating a unique spiritual energy. On this occasion, I bow to all the saints and Gyanis. I extend my greetings to all Sikh brothers and sisters.

Friends,

This year’s Prakash Parv is accompanied by another sacred remembrance. This year marks 350 years since the martyrdom of Sri Guru Tegh Bahadur Ji. It was here on the soil of Delhi that Guru Sahib sacrificed his life for the protection of the identity of this nation, for faith, belief, and values. Three hundred and fifty years have passed since that event, yet the inspiration of that sacrifice remains deeply embedded in the soil of India and in every particle of this nation. The people of this country will always remain grateful to him.

Friends,

The spirit of sacrificing everything for the nation and its culture has always been synonymous with the Sikh tradition. The martyrdom of Bhai Mati Das Ji, Bhai Sati Das Ji, and Bhai Dayala Ji reminds us of this. This spirit continued uninterrupted even during the struggle for Independence. As our Lieutenant Governor explained in detail, Sardar Teja Singh Samundri Ji himself is a shining example of this tradition. He played an important role in the Gurdwara Reform Movement. He dedicated his entire life to service. This year also marks one hundred years since his martyrdom. On this occasion, I respectfully pay tribute to him as well.

Friends,

India endured centuries of slavery. During the most difficult periods in our history, our Gurus kept the flame of protecting the nation burning. Today, when great national resolutions stand before us, when the goal of a Developed India lies ahead of us, this journey can be completed only through the inspiration of our Gurus and the strength of their ideals. Therefore, it is our responsibility to keep Sikh traditions and the heritage of Sikhi shining before society like a beacon of light.

Friends,

Over the past twelve years, we have sincerely tried to fulfil this responsibility. We celebrated the 350th Prakash Parv of Guru Gobind Singh Ji. We celebrated the 550th Prakash Parv of Guru Nanak Dev Ji. Numerous ceremonies were organised across India and abroad. Commemorative coins and postage stamps were issued. Special trains were operated. Programmes dedicated to Gurbani were organised throughout the country. Similarly, on the 400th Prakash Parv of Guru Tegh Bahadur Ji, a national event was held at the Red Fort. With the same spirit, we are currently commemorating the 350th martyrdom anniversary of Guru Tegh Bahadur Ji.

Friends,

One month from now, on 26 December, the country will observe another Veer Baal Diwas. We began observing this day nationally because it is our responsibility to take to the younger generation the immortal saga of the martyrdom of the Sahibzadas, the resolve of Mata Gujri, and that unparalleled courage and valour. When our children learn about the circumstances of those times, when they understand how the Sahibzadas and the Gurus made the supreme sacrifice and how our faith was protected through their sacrifices, that knowledge and inspiration will create youth dedicated to the nation.

Our efforts to honour the Gurus are not limited to India. Just a few days ago, I was in the Philippines. There too, I visited the Khalsa Diwan Gurdwara, the oldest Gurdwara in the Philippines. The Government of India is also supporting the redevelopment of the Khalsa Diwan Gurdwara. Our effort is that the light of our Gurus should reach every person. With their blessings, India should continue to reach new heights.

Friends,

In our Ardas, we say: Nanak Naam Chardi Kala, Tere Bhaane Sarbat Da Bhala. This teaching of wishing well for all comes to us from Gurbani. And the thought of working for everyone’s welfare arises only when we begin to regard everyone as our own.

Friends,

Today, conflicts are taking place in many parts of the world. Many innocent people and families are facing violence and unrest. In such times, the spirit of Sarbat Da Bhala, Sarbat Da Bhala, becomes even more important, so that there may be peace everywhere, every family may remain safe, and every individual may have the opportunity to live with dignity.

Friends,

The greatest test of the values given to us by Guru Sahib comes in our everyday lives. Today, one such challenge before our society is the problem of drugs. Because when a young person falls into the grip of addiction, the dreams of an entire family are shattered along with him.

Friends,

Every young person in the country should be free from addiction. They should fulfil their dreams, study well, progress in life, and bring honour to their family and the nation. In this effort, the role of families is extremely important. Our Sangat, our Gurdwaras, and our social institutions can together guide young people and give them the courage to choose the right path. I am confident that with Guru Sahib’s blessings and the collective efforts of society, we will be able to provide a safe and bright future for our younger generation. With this sentiment, I once again extend lakh-lakh greetings to all of you on the sacred Prakash Parv of Sri Guru Nanak Dev Ji Maharaj.

Waheguru Ji Ka Khalsa, Waheguru Ji Ki Fateh!

Come, join me in raising the Jaikara – Bole So Nihal! Sat Sri Akal!

Thank you very much!
